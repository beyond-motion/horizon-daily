### [Mistral releases Mistral Large 4 weights under restricted license](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-large-4-oss/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Mistral has released the weights of Mistral Large 4, including Hugging Face repositories for Mistral-Large-4-1T-A52B and an NVFP4 variant. The release&\#x27;s &quot;open weight&quot; framing is contested: the license requires a licensee or affiliate that operates a model-as-a-service business to enter a separate commercial license with Mistral before using the model or derivatives for any commercial purpose. Mistral also cut API pricing for the full release to $0.70 per million input tokens and $2.10 per million output tokens, down from $1.36 and $4.18 for the preview, according to a commenter citing Mistral&\#x27;s pricing page. A quoted benchmark claims an average 96.6% refusal rate on malicious cyber requests, described as the highest among open models, while a commenter asks where the non-malicious-request refusal statistic is.

hackernews · kuberwastaken · Oct 29, 14:13 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50289418)

**「Background」** Open-weight models publish their trained parameters, but that does not necessarily make them open source in the OSI sense; Mistral Large 4’s weights are on Hugging Face under the Mistral Ecosystem Open-Weight License, described as a modified MIT license. Mistral Large 4 \(Le Chonk\) is a roughly 1-trillion-parameter multimodal mixture-of-experts model, following a public preview that began on 6 October 2026, when open weights were promised by the end of that month. The distinction matters because the license terms, rather than the availability of weights alone, determine how freely companies can use the model commercially.

**「Impact」** The release mainly benefits teams that self-host Mistral Large 4 or use it through Mistral’s API, while providers operating a Model-as-a-Service business must obtain a separate commercial license before using the weights or derivatives commercially; the roughly halved API pricing \($0.7/$2.1 per million input/output tokens versus the preview\) may justify fresh evaluations.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters challenged the &quot;open weight&quot; label as misleading given the commercial-license requirement, while an EU-based provider reported positive experience using Mistral Large 4 alongside GLM 5.3 Flash and DeepSeek v4.1 Flash 1017 through Mistral&\#x27;s API. Others focused on the roughly halved pricing and the Hugging Face weight links, and one questioned the cyber-refusal benchmark&\#x27;s missing non-malicious-request statistic.

### [DoorDash announces plan to acquire Instacart](https://about.doordash.com/en-us/news/doordash-to-acquire-instacart) ⭐️ 7.0/10

DoorDash announced plans to acquire Instacart, consolidating two large delivery-marketplace brands. The supplied announcement gives no financial terms or timeline. If completed, the deal would combine DoorDash’s restaurant-delivery business with Instacart’s grocery focus; one commenter noted that the overlap is less direct than it appears because DoorDash has little grocery-delivery presence and Instacart’s non-grocery deliveries are powered by Uber Eats. The transaction is likely to draw antitrust scrutiny and matters to merchants and consumers because it could reshape competition in local delivery against Walmart and Amazon.

hackernews · WalterSobchak · Oct 29, 12:33 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50287919)

**「Background」** DoorDash is a major restaurant-delivery platform, while Instacart is a leading online grocery-delivery marketplace; the proposed deal would combine those two roles. Reports put the transaction at $17 billion, and Instacart&\#x27;s investor release values its shares at $65.00, a 26% premium to its October 22, 2026 closing price. The companies frame the combination as helping local stores compete with large online retailers and grocers.

**「Impact」** If completed, the deal—reported to value Instacart at roughly $17 billion—would combine DoorDash’s delivery network with Instacart’s grocery marketplace, potentially intensifying competition with Walmart and Amazon in grocery delivery.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters were split: several initially questioned antitrust but noted the companies’ limited product overlap, while others argued antitrust enforcement is ineffective or asked whether approval should impose conditions on a platform that both coordinates and competes in local delivery. Merchants in the thread described DoorDash as a necessary but disliked partner, citing commissions and driver conduct, and another commenter called its app experience full of dark patterns.
