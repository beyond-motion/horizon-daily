### [What Sun Got Wrong: A Retrospective Analysis](https://bcantrill.dtrace.org/2026/09/20/what-sun-got-wrong/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A retrospective post titled &quot;What Sun got wrong&quot; on bcantrill.dtrace.org analyzes Sun Microsystems&\#x27; strategic mistakes and was submitted to Hacker News by chmaynard. The item&\#x27;s analysis focuses on hardware commoditization, cloud computing, and engineering-versus-sales dynamics as pressures that shaped Sun&\#x27;s fate. The accompanying Hacker News thread is historical industry reflection rather than breaking news, and no full article text was supplied with the source item. The discussion adds firsthand perspectives on enterprise hardware procurement and the shift to commodified, on-demand compute.

hackernews · chmaynard · Sep 21, 14:03 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49787436)

**「Background」** Sun Microsystems was an American technology company that developed and sold computers, hardware, software, and information technology services from 1982 until 2010, and its servers became a staple of corporate data centers. By early 2009 its position had weakened enough that IBM reportedly weighed acquiring the server maker for nearly $7 billion, a marker of the pressure that hardware commoditization and the shift toward on-demand cloud computing were placing on proprietary systems vendors. This item is a retrospective analysis of the strategic choices Sun got wrong, and the attached discussion focuses on its sales model, engineering culture, and the economics of its hardware business.

**「Impact」** The most concrete consequence for Sun&\#x27;s customers and the broader proprietary-systems market was strategic vulnerability: as commodity hardware and on-demand compute squeezed incumbent server vendors, Sun itself became an acquisition target, with I.B.M. exploring a purchase of the company for nearly $7 billion in 2009.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters broadly agreed that Sun&\#x27;s engineering strengths were undercut by a cumbersome sales and procurement model and by the rise of commodity compute, though one argued the engineering-versus-sales framing is too one-sided because the sales perspective is absent. Others shared firsthand experience: Sun and DEC hardware buying was far more expensive and slower than Dell, Sun thin clients were slow but beloved by hacker-type users, and one commenter recalled selling Sun stock at $70 before it fell to $7 and compared that to high valuations for Tesla, SpaceX, and AI stocks.

### [Grok 4.7 Release Draws Pricing and Benchmark Debate](https://x.ai/news/grok-4-7) ⭐️ 7.0/10

xAI has released Grok 4.7, an incremental update to its frontier model, but the supplied item does not include the official release details. In Hacker News discussion, commenter moojacob said Grok 4.7 has 40% more weights than Grok 4.6 while keeping the same $2 input and $6 output token prices, and that xAI delayed the release by almost two weeks past its original date. Commenters also noted the timing came just before the rumored launch of Anthropic&\#x27;s Opus 5.5, fueling skepticism about xAI&\#x27;s competitive position and benchmarks. Simon Willison reported inconsistent token usage across reasoning-effort settings, while others debated whether xAI remains merely &quot;just behind&quot; OpenAI and Anthropic or is being outpaced by alternatives such as Astra.

hackernews · meetpateltech · Sep 21, 15:50 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49788838)

**「Background」** Grok is a series of generative large language models developed by xAI, launched in November 2023 as an initiative by Elon Musk. Grok 4.7 is the newest release in that line, which xAI describes as its most capable model for coding and knowledge work, offered at the same price and speed as Grok 4.6. It arrives during a crowded frontier-model race in which xAI has generally been seen as trailing OpenAI and Anthropic, with Anthropic&\#x27;s Opus 5.5 rumored to be launching around the same time.

**「Impact」** For developers and teams evaluating frontier models, Grok 4.7&\#x27;s unchanged token pricing alongside a reported 40% weight increase may lower the cost of trying the model, but the community&\#x27;s benchmark skepticism and reported reasoning-level token inconsistencies mean practical, task-specific testing is more useful than headline scores.

**「Community Discussion」** Hacker News commenters disagreed on the release&\#x27;s significance: some welcomed the faster cadence and predicted a larger Grok 5 step later this year, while others said xAI remains behind OpenAI, Anthropic, and Astra. A recurring practical concern was evaluation quality, including Simon Willison&\#x27;s finding that reasoning effort low and medium used similar token counts and xhigh used fewer tokens than high.
