### [PBT doesn’t speed grokking, but can escape a dead-zone range](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1xfqhfc/pbt_doesnt_make_grokking_faster_but_it_can_escape/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A Reddit-reported experiment tested Population-Based Training \(PBT\) over learning rate and weight decay on grokking with modular addition mod 97, a 35% train split, a small MLP, 20 population members, 20k full-batch steps, and equal compute against a grid over 3 seeds. The best fixed grid cell reached 95% validation accuracy in 317 ± 29 steps, while PBT took 2667 ± 2244 steps and was slower in every seed, showing PBT did not accelerate grokking when the grid already contained a good corner. From a dead-zone initialization \(lr 1e-4 to 1e-3, wd 0.01 to 0.08\) where no fixed setting grokked, fixed settings and slow PBT both scored 0/3, but fast PBT resampled every 100 steps with ×0.5/×2 multipliers reached 3/3 at about 6,850 steps; a random donor still escaped later, and without selection the population grokked then lost it. A 10-seed wide random search hit 95% in a median 300 steps versus 6,650 for PBT from the dead zone, PBT sometimes collapsed into the lr-floor/wd-ceiling corner, and replaying the winning lr/wd schedule without PBT reached 55/60 on fresh inits versus 0/60 for constant knobs at the schedule’s geometric mean, indicating the schedule rather than weight copying carried the result. The author frames PBT as a rescue tool for a bad hyperparameter range rather than a speed-up, caveating that results cover one task, model, and split, use 3 to 10 seeds with p-values mostly above 0.05, rely on post-hoc winning schedules, and do not test a range with no grokking region at all.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/Virtual-Message-9739 · Oct 26, 07:48

**「Background」** Grokking is a training phenomenon in which a model fits its training data early but generalizes to held-out data only much later; it was originally studied on modular addition, where small models can suddenly reach high validation accuracy after prolonged training. Population-based training \(PBT\) is a hyperparameter-optimization method that trains a population of models, periodically copies weights from better-performing members, and perturbs their hyperparameters such as learning rate and weight decay. These concepts matter here because the experiment compares PBT with fixed grid and random search on a modular-addition grokking task.

**「Impact」** Practitioners should treat PBT as a fallback for escaping poorly chosen ranges, not as a faster alternative to grid or random search when a grokking region is already reachable.
