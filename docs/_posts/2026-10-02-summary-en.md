### [Court agrees with EFF that Utah&\#x27;s VPN law is technically impossible](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/10/court-agrees-eff-utahs-vpn-law-demands-technical-impossibility) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A court has agreed with the Electronic Frontier Foundation \(EFF\) that Utah&\#x27;s VPN law imposes requirements that are technically impossible to satisfy, according to an EFF analysis. The law would leave platforms with an impossible choice: completely block all VPN traffic nationwide or withdraw access from Utah entirely, as quoted in the Hacker News discussion. The case matters because it tests whether a state can compel online services to detect and block VPN use, a capability that is technically unreliable and central to debates over privacy, censorship, and jurisdiction.

hackernews · hn\_acker · Oct 1, 22:23 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49927754)

**「Background」** Utah&\#x27;s law required covered commercial entities to implement &quot;commercially reasonable geolocation obfuscation detection systems&quot; and barred websites from providing instructions on using a VPN to bypass those checks. EFF challenged the administrative rules as demanding a technical impossibility, and a federal court preliminarily enjoined the VPN provisions. The court found the law likely violates the U.S. Constitution&\#x27;s prohibition on state laws that significantly burden businesses and people outside Utah&\#x27;s borders.

**「Impact」** The court&\#x27;s agreement with EFF undercuts Utah&\#x27;s ability to enforce the VPN-blocking requirement as written, easing the choice between nationwide VPN blocking and withdrawing from Utah—though the ruling&\#x27;s precise scope is not detailed in the available summary.

**「Community Discussion」** Several commenters welcomed the outcome but questioned whether VPN traffic can be reliably detected, with one noting that anyone can proxy through a random hosting provider. Others debated jurisdiction—arguing that a state has no business regulating servers outside its borders—and whether the familiar claim that the internet routes around censorship still holds given crackdowns in Iran, China, and Kashmir; some framed the law as part of a broader authoritarian push beyond adult content.

### [arXiv limits submitters to two submissions per calendar month](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/10/01/updated-rate-limit-policy/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

arXiv has updated its rate-limit policy to allow submitters up to two submissions per calendar month, according to a blog post dated October 1, 2026. The change is a significant operational policy shift for research publishing, directly affecting how AI/ML and other researchers disseminate preprints. The supplied source content was unavailable, so details such as enforcement mechanics, exceptions, or whether revised versions count are not confirmed in the provided material. Community reaction focused on the policy&\#x27;s potential to curb paper mills and submission spam while raising questions about whether large labs can evade the cap by rotating authors.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · Nunki08 · Oct 2, 00:47 · [Discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wvg7yc/arxiv_now_limits_submitters_to_up_to_two/)

**「Background」** arXiv is a preprint server where researchers share papers before peer review, and its updated rate limit policy caps submitters at two submissions per calendar month with three active submissions at any time. The change, effective immediately, is intended to equitably distribute volunteer moderators&\#x27; time and protect the corpus from a sharp increase in inappropriate submissions, including AI-generated or low-quality work. The limit applies across all arXiv categories, not just computer science, and a rejected submission still counts against the monthly total.

**「Impact」** Submitters—especially large labs and prolific authors who previously posted many preprints per month—must now triage their releases to fit two submissions per calendar month and no more than three active submissions at any given time, a constraint arXiv says is intended to distribute volunteer moderators&\#x27; time more equitably across authors.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters broadly welcomed the cap as a break from low-quality preprint volume, with several arguing it should also constrain prolific lab PIs and that journals should adopt similar quotas, citing TMLR. Others doubted enforcement, predicting paper mills will rotate authors, asked whether version updates count, and warned about workarounds such as very long papers or that preprint culture needs a new paradigm.
